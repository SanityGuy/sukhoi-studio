An experimental technology demonstrator featuring revolutionary forward-swept wings, extensive composite construction, and extreme low-speed agility.

Forward-Swept Aerodynamics & Design

The Sukhoi Su-47 Berkut (Golden Eagle), initially designated S-37, was an experimental supersonic jet fighter demonstrator developed by Sukhoi. Designed to explore advanced fly-by-wire flight control systems and high-agility wing geometry, its defining characteristic was its radical forward-swept wings (FSW).

The aircraft made its inaugural flight on 25 September 1997. The forward-swept configuration provided superior lift-to-drag ratios, reduced takeoff and landing distances, and outstanding subsonic maneuverability at high angles of attack. Structural data and composite manufacturing techniques gathered from the Berkut program directly laid the foundation for Russia's 5th-generation Su-57 fighter.

Experimental Technology & Systems

System Description
Forward-Swept Wing Geometry Provides unexcelled low-speed agility and stall resistance by delaying airflow separation at high angles of attack compared to aft-swept wings.
Fly-By-Wire Flight Controls Multi-channel digital fly-by-wire system compensating for extreme aerodynamic instability and continuously adjusting control surface angles.
Composite Airframe Structure Extensive structural use of high-strength carbon-fiber composite panels woven to withstand twisting forces generated along the wing structure.
Internal Weapon Carriage Early prototype internal weapons bay positioned along the ventral centerline to reduce radar cross-section (RCS) during stealth tests.
Advanced Pilot Interface Equipped with a high-G inclined ejection seat (30 degrees) and side-stick fly-by-wire control layout designed to assist pilot g-tolerance.
