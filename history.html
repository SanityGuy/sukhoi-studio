THE SUKHOI LEGACY

Founded by Pavel Sukhoi in 1939, the bureau has spent nearly a century driving tactical aviation forward. From early piston-engine bombers to Cold War interceptors and modern thrust-vectoring fighters, this history reflects a relentless pursuit of speed and extreme maneuverability.

Explore the History

Pavel Sukhoi's Legacy

The Sukhoi Design Bureau (OKB-51) was founded in 1939 by Pavel Sukhoi. Early aircraft like the Su-2 light bomber laid the foundation for the bureau's deep integration with Soviet aviation forces. However, it was during the Cold War that Sukhoi truly began to define modern combat aviation.

Following Pavel Sukhoi's pioneering work, the bureau focused heavily on high-speed aerodynamics, variable-geometry wings, and unparalleled maneuverability. The creation of the Su-27 Flanker series in the late 1970s firmly established the bureau as a global leader in air superiority fighters.

Evolutionary Milestones

Year Aircraft Significance
1939 Sukhoi Design Bureau Pavel Sukhoi established the Sukhoi Experimental Design Bureau (OKB-51), marking the beginning of one of the Soviet Union's most influential aircraft manufacturers.
1941-1945 World War II The Su-2 light bomber served with the Soviet Air Force during the early years of World War II, becoming Sukhoi's first combat aircraft.
1960s Cold War Expansion Sukhoi developed advanced supersonic fighters including the Su-7, Su-9, Su-11, and Su-15 to defend Soviet airspace during the Cold War.
1975 T-10 Program Development of the T-10 prototype began to counter the American F-15 Eagle, eventually evolving into the Su-27 Flanker.
1977 Su-27 Prototype The T-10 prototype completed its maiden flight, beginning development of the legendary Flanker family.
1985 Su-27 Enters Service The Su-27 officially entered Soviet service, becoming one of the world's premier air superiority fighters.
1989 Pugachev's Cobra Test pilot Viktor Pugachev stunned the aviation world at the Paris Air Show by performing the famous "Pugachev's Cobra" maneuver with the Su-27.
1991 Soviet Union Dissolution Following the collapse of the Soviet Union, Sukhoi transitioned into the Russian aerospace industry and expanded international exports.
1997 Su-47 Berkut The experimental Su-47 demonstrated forward-swept wing technology and advanced fly-by-wire control systems, influencing future fighter research.
2008 Su-35S The extensively modernized Su-35 made its first flight, featuring thrust-vectoring engines, advanced avionics, and the Irbis-E radar.
2010 Su-57 Maiden Flight The fifth-generation Su-57 prototype (T-50) completed its first flight, introducing stealth features and next-generation avionics.
2019 First Production Su-57 The first production Su-57 was completed, although it was lost during a pre-delivery test flight due to a control system malfunction.
2020 Su-57 Enters Service The Russian Aerospace Forces officially accepted the first operational Su-57, marking the beginning of fifth-generation fighter service.
2021-Present Modern Operations Sukhoi aircraft including the Su-30SM, Su-34, Su-35S, and Su-57 continue serving with multiple air forces and remain among Russia's flagship combat aircraft.
